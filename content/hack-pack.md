## 3. The Three Tracks

Pick **one**. Your track frames how you're judged. The through-line for all three: **do real work for the real world, and get paid.**

### 01 · Outbuild

Find a slow, bloated, or half-built product — even tools you use daily have broken corners (yes, even Luma). Ship the sharper version, ideally **open source**, and go get **real users and actual paychecks**.

> **Example:** Pick a product with obvious gaps. Rebuild the part that's broken, open-source it, and pull over as many real (ideally paying) users as you can in 36 hours.
>
> **What wins:** clearly better than the incumbent · real users switched · ideally revenue.

### 02 · Validate

Build the evidence pack against a company and **show where the story doesn't hold** — where claims, numbers, or reality don't line up. Rigorous, credible, data-backed.

> **Example:** Take a company making claims that don't add up. Build the dashboard / report / analysis that exposes the gap — and makes the truth impossible to ignore.
>
> **What wins:** rigour and credibility · the significance of the truth you surface · proof a decision-maker can't argue with.

### 03 · Effect

Find one real person or organisation and **remove a recurring burden *with* them — not *for* them.** Hackers can genuinely transform someone's week. Stale industries and businesses that barely use their own software (let alone AI) will benefit enormously. Then **get paid.**

> **Example:** Find a real person, or a business in a stale industry, drowning in a repetitive burden. Sit with them, build the thing that removes it — keeping them in the loop — and get paid for it. A local business, a solo professional, anyone whose life you can visibly change.
>
> **What wins:** a real burden visibly removed for a *named* person/org · money changed hands · genuine collaboration *with* them.

## 6. Judging Criteria

We judge on **evidence, not promises.** Every team is scored against a shared baseline, then on what matters most for *your* track.

### Baseline (all tracks)

- **Does it actually work?** Live, real, demonstrable — not a mockup.
- **Depth of proof.** Show the receipts (see Submission, §7).
- **Honesty.** Real numbers only.

### Track-specific weighting


| Track        | What we weight most                                                                                                      |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Outbuild** | How much *better* than the incumbent · real users switched over · revenue / paychecks earned                             |
| **Validate** | Rigour & credibility of the evidence · how significant the truth you exposed is · would it hold up to scrutiny           |
| **Effect**   | Realness of the burden removed for a *named* person/org · money earned · the "*with* them" collaboration, not "for" them |


### ⭐ Bonus points

- **Build in public** — posting your progress during the hack (§5).

## 14. Code of Conduct

Pop the Bubble is built on respect, generosity, and good-faith building. This applies **everywhere** the event lives — the venue, Discord, the awards reception, and anything you post under the event's name.

**We expect you to:**

- Treat everyone — builders, organisers, mentors, sponsors, venue staff, guests — with respect, regardless of background, identity, or experience level.
- Be generous: share knowledge, help when asked, lift others up.
- Build honestly. No faked work, traction, or proof (see §4).
- Respect the venue and equipment. Clean up after yourself. It's not yours.
- Respect people's focus, sleep, and consent — including for photos and filming (we film the event; tell an organiser if you'd rather not be on camera).

**We will not tolerate:**

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or hate speech of any kind — including in DMs and on socials.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or contact.
- Sustained disruption, bullying, or deliberate sabotage of other teams.
- Plagiarism or passing off others' work as your own.
- Bringing weapons, illegal substances, or anything that endangers others.

**Reporting:** if something's wrong, tell **any organiser in person** or **DM a mod in Discord**. We take every report seriously and will keep it confidential.

**Consequences:** organisers may issue a warning, remove you from the event or Discord, disqualify your team, or involve venue security — without refund of any perks — depending on severity. Organisers' decisions are final.

The short version: **be excellent to each other, and don't be the reason we have to write more rules.**

## 15. FAQ

**Can I start building before the event?**
Prep all you want — plan, research, build context, line up warm leads, talk to your target customer. That's encouraged. But the **execution happens during the 36 hours.** A little pre-building is fine; the bar is simple: **the work you do during the hack must be so substantial that no one can deny it.** If the weekend's progress is the obvious heart of the project, you're good.

**Do I keep my IP?** 100%. It's entirely yours.

**Can I join solo and find a team there?** Yes — Friday's arrival window is for exactly this. Start early in **#team-formation**.

**Is there somewhere to sleep?** No dedicated rest zone, but the building's big and there are plenty of couches. **Bring a sleeping bag** (§12).

**What if I get stuck?** Post in **#help**, tag a mentor/sponsor in Discord, or find them on-site (§10).

**How do I win?** Real evidence of impact against your track — money, users, burdens removed. Plus build in public for bonus points. Not promises.
